# Final Reflection

## 1. Looking back across all 15 days, what is the single most valuable skill you built?

The most valuable skill I built during this internship was learning how to turn raw data into useful business insights. Before this project, I mainly thought of data analysis as working with numbers and charts. During the internship, I learned the full process, starting from cleaning the data and building the data model, to creating DAX measures, designing a Power BI dashboard, checking insights, and finally presenting the results. I think being able to explain what the numbers actually mean for a business is the most useful skill I gained.

## 2. If you had one more week, what would you improve first, and why?

If I had one more week, I would improve the Power BI dashboard further and add more advanced analysis. I would especially like to add forecasting and more detailed analysis of regional and product performance. I would also spend more time improving the dashboard layout based on feedback from a real business user. This would make the project more useful for understanding what could happen in the future instead of mainly looking at past performance.

## 3. How would you now explain, in under 2 minutes, what this entire project was for and what it found — to someone who has never seen it?

This project was created to understand the business performance of the Global Superstore dataset and turn the data into information that could support business decisions. I cleaned and organized the data, created a Power BI data model, built measures and then created a four-page dashboard covering overall performance, sales, customers, and products.

The analysis showed that the company generated around $12.64 million in sales and $1.47 million in profit. The Consumer segment was the biggest contributor, generating around $6.5 million or 51.48% of total sales. Technology was one of the strongest categories, with the highest sales and around a 14% profit margin, while Furniture had a lower margin of around 7%. Sales also changed throughout the year, with November and December being stronger months and February being one of the weaker months.

Based on these findings, I would recommend continuing to focus on Consumer customers, maintaining the strong performance of Technology, improving Furniture profitability, and planning marketing and inventory around the seasonal changes in sales.
